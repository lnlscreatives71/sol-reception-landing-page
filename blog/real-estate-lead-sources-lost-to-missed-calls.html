7 lead sources that call real estate agents (and what each one costs you when you miss it)

October 5, 2026 · 5 min read · Sol Reception

You paid for the sign rider, the Zillow leads, the open house, the Google profile, and the years of goodwill that make past clients refer you. Every one of those ends the same way: a person picks up a phone and calls. If you are showing a home, in a listing appointment, or asleep, that call goes to voicemail, and the lead goes to the next agent.

This list walks through the seven places real estate calls come from, what each one is worth, and what a missed call costs you on each.

Short answer: The real estate leads agents lose most to missed calls are yard sign calls, portal leads from sites like Zillow, open house follow-ups, Google Business Profile calls, past-client referrals, after-hours buyer inquiries, and Spanish-speaking callers. Each one is a lead you already paid to generate, and the agent who answers first usually keeps it. A bilingual AI receptionist like Sol answers every call live on the first ring, in English and Spanish, qualifies the caller, and books the next step while you stay with the client in front of you.

01

Yard sign and listing flyer calls

Someone driving by sees your sign, likes the house, and calls the number right then. Their interest is highest in that moment, and they are often calling from the curb.

If you miss that call, they do not leave a voicemail. They pull up the listing on their phone, see another agent's name on a similar home, and call that one. A sign call is a warm lead you did not have to chase, and it is one of the easiest to lose because it comes at random times, often while you are with another client.

What a miss costs: a ready buyer who may have been the only interested person at that moment, plus a seller who sees your sign generating fewer inquiries than it should.

02

Zillow, Realtor.com, and other portal leads

Portal leads are the most expensive calls on this list because you pay per lead or per click. They are also the least patient. The person filled out a form on several agents' pages, and the first agent to reach them tends to win the conversation.

If your response takes an hour, you are usually calling someone who has already spoken to another agent. The phone call is where that race gets decided, and speed beats polish.

What a miss costs: the full price of the lead, plus the deal. If you pay for a portal lead that never turns into a conversation, you are paying for the privilege of losing it.

03

Open house follow-up calls

Someone signs in at your open house on Sunday and calls Monday with a question about the offer process, the HOA, or whether the sellers would take a lower price. That call is a buying signal. It comes while you are back at work, with other clients, or on another showing.

When the call rings out, the person assumes you are not interested in their business. Another agent's open house is probably next weekend.

What a miss costs: the conversion of your best event-based lead source, which cost you a weekend and the seller's patience to produce.

04

Google Business Profile and map calls

A growing number of people find agents by searching “real estate agent near me” and tapping the call button on a map listing. These callers have not been to your site and are not comparing credentials. They are tapping and calling.

That call button is a direct line from search to your phone. If it goes to voicemail, you have paid for reviews, listings, and a profile that did its job and then dropped the hand-off.

What a miss costs: a lead whose entire decision window is the next two minutes, which is the lead your online presence worked hardest to earn.

05

Past-client and sphere referrals

Referrals are your best leads because the trust is already built. A past client tells a friend, “Call my agent.” The friend calls, and nobody picks up.

Referral callers are warm but not loyal to you yet. They know one name and a general reputation. If voicemail answers, many of them search for someone else, and your best source of free business loses to a missed ring.

What a miss costs: the highest-converting lead type you have, and the awkward moment when the past client hears their friend never reached you.

06

After-hours and weekend buyer calls

A lot of home searching happens at night and on weekends, when people have time to browse and talk to their partner. Calls come in at 8 p.m. on a Tuesday or Saturday afternoon while you are at your kid's game.

Out-of-area relocators add another wrinkle. Someone planning a move from another time zone may call at hours that make no sense for you, and they expect an answer.

What a miss costs: buyers who are serious enough to call outside business hours, who are often exactly the motivated ones, and who will not wait until Monday morning.

07

Spanish-speaking buyer and seller calls

In many markets, Spanish-speaking buyers and sellers are a large and growing part of the pool, and they often prefer to start the conversation in Spanish. If the only person who answers cannot switch languages, many callers hang up and try someone else.

Many agents never see this loss, because the caller leaves no voicemail and never shows up in a report. It is a lead that simply did not happen.

What a miss costs: a share of your market you may not realize you are missing, and every referral that would have come from that client afterward.

What do all seven have in common?

They all arrive at the worst possible time for you, and they are all easy to lose to the first agent who answers. You are not missing calls because you are lazy. You are missing them because your day is made of showings, listing appointments, and closings, and none of those can pause for the phone.
