8 questions to ask before hiring an AI receptionist (2026 buyer's checklist)

July 22, 2026 · 7 min read · Sol Reception

An AI receptionist is only worth buying if it does the one job that matters: turning a ringing phone into a booked appointment or a captured lead, every time, without sending your customer to voicemail or a dead end.

Most of them can answer a call. Far fewer can actually book the job, hand off to a human cleanly, or handle a Spanish-speaking caller. Before you pay for one, ask the eight questions below. They separate a tool that fills your calendar from one that just talks.

This matters whether you run a med spa, a dental office, a chiropractic clinic, a real estate business, or a home services company. You all live and die by the phone, and a missed or fumbled call is lost revenue you never see. Here is what to ask.

01

Does it actually book into my calendar, or just take a message?

This is the difference between a receptionist and a glorified voicemail. A traditional answering service takes a message and hands it back to you to chase later. Ask whether the AI books directly into your scheduling system, whether that is your practice management software, Google Calendar, Calendly, Housecall Pro, or Jobber. A med spa owner does not want twelve messages to return at closing time. She wants twelve appointments already on the books.

02

What happens when it cannot handle a call?

Every AI hits a question it was not built for. What matters is what it does next. A good one says something like “I do not have that specific detail, but I will have someone from the team call you right back,” then captures the lead and routes it to a human. A bad one guesses, invents an answer, or dead-ends the caller. Ask exactly how human handoff works, and whether an urgent call, say a plumbing emergency or a patient in pain, can be routed to your on-call person immediately.

03

Can it answer callers in Spanish?

More than 43 million people in the US speak Spanish at home, and in many markets a real share of your inbound calls are Spanish-speaking. If your phone answerer only works in English, those callers hang up and dial a competitor who can help them. Contractors and clinics without Spanish support can miss 15% to 25% of potential customers in Hispanic-heavy areas. Ask whether the AI handles both English and Spanish on the same line, automatically, so you capture that market without hiring bilingual staff.

04

Does it answer 24/7, including nights and weekends?

The whole reason to add an AI receptionist is to catch the calls your team physically cannot. For a lot of businesses, more than half of calls come outside standard hours. A homeowner's power goes out on a Sunday. A prospective patient researches dentists at 9 p.m. and calls the first one who picks up. Ask whether coverage is genuinely around the clock, or whether it quietly falls back to voicemail after hours, which defeats the purpose.

05

Can I hear it before I buy? Ask for a live test call.

Never buy an AI receptionist you have not heard answer a real scenario. Ask to place a live test call using your actual business situation, a new-patient booking, an after-hours emergency, a pricing question. If the caller can instantly tell they are talking to a clumsy robot, it will cost you brand trust and bookings. You want a conversation that sounds warm and natural, not a phone tree wearing a costume.

06

How does it learn my business?

An AI receptionist is only as good as what it knows about you. Ask whether you can feed it your website, your FAQs, your service list, your pricing, and your hours, and how quickly it picks up changes. A real estate agent wants it to know which listings are active and how to qualify a buyer. A chiropractor wants it to know which insurance you take and what a new-patient visit involves. If it cannot learn your specifics, it will frustrate the exact callers you most want to keep.

07

Does it back up my team, or is it a full replacement?

The best setups treat the AI as backup, not a replacement for your front desk. It should catch overflow when your staff is with a client and cover the hours no one is at the desk, then get out of the way when a human is the right answer. Be wary of any pitch that says you can fire your receptionist. Your team handles the nuanced, high-touch moments. The AI makes sure nothing goes to voicemail while they do. Sol is built exactly this way: it backs up your people, it never replaces them.

08

What are the contract terms, and what happens during an outage?

Read the fine print before you sign. Ask whether you are locked into a long contract or can cancel month to month, where your call data is stored and who can access it, and what the system does if it ever goes down. There should be a clean failover, to voicemail or a human, so a technical hiccup never means a silently dropped call. A tool that captures leads should never become the reason you lose one.

FAQ

Common questions

What is the most important thing to look for in an AI receptionist?

The single most important thing is whether it books the appointment or captures the lead, not just answers the phone. Many tools can hold a conversation, but far fewer complete the booking into your calendar and hand off cleanly to a human when needed. If it only takes a message, it is closer to voicemail than to a receptionist.

Should an AI receptionist replace my front desk staff?

No. The best setups use the AI as backup, catching overflow calls when staff are busy and covering nights and weekends, while your team handles the high-touch moments. A tool that promises to fully replace your front desk usually cannot deliver the nuance real customers need, so treat backup, not replacement, as the goal.

How do I test an AI receptionist before buying?

Ask to place a live test call using a real scenario from your business, such as a new-patient booking or an after-hours emergency. Listen for whether it sounds natural, asks the right questions, books correctly, and hands off to a human when it should. If callers can immediately tell it is a clumsy robot, it will cost you bookings and trust.

Can an AI receptionist answer calls in Spanish?

Yes, the good ones do. With more than 43 million Spanish speakers in the US, an English-only phone answerer can miss a large share of callers in many markets. Sol answers in both English and Spanish on the same line automatically, so you capture those customers without hiring bilingual staff.

What should happen when the AI cannot answer a caller's question?

It should admit it does not have the detail, capture the caller's information, and route the lead to a human rather than guessing or inventing an answer. For urgent calls, it should hand off to your on-call person immediately. Ask any vendor to walk you through exactly how this handoff works before you buy.
