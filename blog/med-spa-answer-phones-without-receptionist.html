7 ways to answer your med spa's phone without a full-time receptionist

September 14, 2026 · 6 min read · Sol Reception

You can answer every med spa call without a full-time receptionist by combining online booking with a live answering layer, either a shared-duty system, an answering service, a virtual receptionist, or an AI receptionist that picks up whenever your team cannot. A full-time front desk hire runs $35,000 to $45,000 a year plus benefits, with the Bureau of Labor Statistics putting median receptionist pay at $38,010 a year as of May 2025, which is hard to justify for a solo injector or a two-room spa. But the phone still rings, and it rings most while you are mid-treatment with gloves on. Here are seven real options, with the honest tradeoffs of each.

01

Share phone duty across your providers

The default for most small spas: whoever is free grabs the phone. It costs nothing on paper, which is why it is so common, and why its real cost stays hidden. When your injector answers between clients, you are paying a provider's hourly rate for reception work, and when nobody is free, which is most of the day in a busy spa, calls simply go unanswered. A caller pricing Botox consultations is usually calling two or three spas in a row; the one that answers gets the consult.

Run one honest test before trusting this system: check your phone log for a single week and count the calls that rang out or hit voicemail during treatment hours. Industry estimates suggest owners who do this commonly find they are missing 20 to 40 percent of daytime calls without ever feeling it, because a missed call makes no sound in the room where you are working.

02

Voicemail plus fast callbacks

Better than nothing, but only slightly. Many callers, especially younger clients booking aesthetics for the first time, will not leave a voicemail at all. They hang up and tap the next result. Even with disciplined same-hour callbacks, you are converting the minority who left a message, and you are calling back people who may have already booked elsewhere. Voicemail works as a safety net behind another answering layer, not as your front line.

03

Missed-call text-back

An automatic “Sorry we missed you, how can we help?” text sent to every missed caller. It is cheap, easy to set up, and rescues a real slice of would-be lost callers, because it opens a channel before they finish dialing your competitor. Its limit is that it depends on the caller to keep engaging, and it cannot answer questions. Someone who wants to know whether you offer lip filler this Saturday still has to wait for a human to text back, and if that reply comes two hours later, the Saturday slot conversation is probably happening with another spa. Good supplement, weak foundation.

04

Lean on online booking as your front door

Every med spa should have online booking, and pushing clients toward it absorbs a chunk of routine scheduling. But the calls that remain are exactly the ones online booking cannot handle: “Which treatment is right for my skin?”, “Can I come in today?”, “Do you have financing?”, and the nervous first-timer who wants to hear a voice before trusting you with their face. Those callers are your highest-value new clients, and a booking link does not talk them through the door.

05

Hire a traditional answering service

A call center answers as your spa and takes messages, typically $100 to $300 a month depending on volume. You get a live human on every call, which beats voicemail decisively. The weakness is depth: most generalist services read from a thin script, cannot see your calendar, and know nothing about the difference between a HydraFacial and a chemical peel. Callers get a polite message-taker when what they wanted was to book. You still do the calling back, and the booking still is not made. If you go this route, insist on a service that will at least capture treatment interest and preferred appointment times, so your callbacks start warm instead of cold.

06

Bring on a part-time or virtual receptionist

A remote receptionist working set hours, often $15 to $25 an hour, can book directly into your software and learn your treatments properly. For spas with predictable call patterns, this is a solid middle step. The gaps are coverage and continuity: they work their hours, not your callers' hours, so evenings, weekends, lunch rushes, and sick days still leak calls. And when a good one leaves, their knowledge of your spa leaves too, and you train the next one from zero. At 20 hours a week, you are also still spending $1,300 to $2,000 a month to cover roughly a quarter of the hours your phone actually rings.

07

Add an AI receptionist as your always-on layer

This is the piece that makes every other option work: a receptionist that answers instantly whenever a call would otherwise be missed, at 8 PM, during a lash lift, on Sunday morning, or while your part-timer is on the other line. A good AI receptionist answers questions about your treatments and pricing ranges, books consultations straight into your calendar, captures the caller's name and number every time, and hands anything sensitive to your team. Sol does this in English and Spanish, which matters in markets where a large share of aesthetics clients prefer Spanish on the phone. And to be clear about the role: Sol is not a replacement for the warm human at your front desk, if you have one. It is the backup that catches everything she physically cannot, so your team spends its time on the clients in the room.

Picture the common case: it is 7:40 PM, you closed at 6, and a woman who just got paid is finally researching the lip filler she has wanted for months. She calls the three spas she found on Google. Two ring out. The third answers, tells her the treatment starts at a price she is comfortable with, and books her consultation for Thursday at 5:15 before the call ends. Nothing about that interaction required a human, and all of it required someone picking up.

How to choose: match the layer to the leak

The right setup depends on where your calls are leaking. If you are missing mostly after-hours calls, an always-on AI layer solves it outright. If you are missing daytime calls because providers are in treatments, you need something that answers while the phone is ringing, which rules out text-back and voicemail as primary fixes. If your callers skew toward complex medical questions, keep a clear escalation path to a human on every option you pick. And if a meaningful share of your market speaks Spanish, make bilingual answering a requirement, not a bonus, because a caller who hits an English-only line often will not call back. Most small spas land on a stack rather than a single tool: online booking for self-servers, text-back as the rescue net, and a live answering layer for everything else.

The math, quickly

A full-time receptionist costs roughly $3,000 to $3,700 a month before benefits. By commonly cited industry figures, a new med spa client is worth $300 to $500 on the first visit and far more over a year of repeat treatments. If missed calls are costing you even three or four new clients a month, you do not have a staffing problem, you have an answering problem, and the answering problem is much cheaper to fix. Combine online booking for the self-servers, missed-call text-back as a rescue net, and an AI receptionist like Sol as the always-on layer, and every caller gets an answer without a single new hire.

FAQ

Common questions

Does a small med spa need a full-time receptionist?

Not necessarily. A full-time front desk hire runs $35,000 to $45,000 a year plus benefits, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics puts median pay for receptionists at $38,010 a year as of May 2025, which many solo injectors and two-room spas cannot justify. What every spa does need is a way for each call to be answered live, which can be assembled from online booking, an answering layer such as an AI receptionist, and clear handoff rules, at a fraction of a salary.

What is the cheapest way for a med spa to stop missing calls?

Missed-call text-back is the cheapest meaningful step: it automatically texts every caller you miss and rescues some before they book elsewhere. It cannot answer questions or book appointments on its own, so most spas pair it with online booking and a live answering layer for full coverage.

What is the difference between an answering service and an AI receptionist for a med spa?

A traditional answering service puts a human message-taker on the line who typically cannot see your calendar or answer treatment questions, so you still return every call. An AI receptionist answers instantly at any hour, discusses your actual treatments and pricing ranges, books consultations directly into your scheduling software, and escalates sensitive calls to your team.

Can an AI receptionist book med spa appointments directly?

Yes. A properly configured AI receptionist connects to your booking software, offers real open slots during the call, and confirms the appointment before hanging up. It also captures the caller's name, number, and treatment interest on every call, so nothing arrives as an anonymous missed number.

Will clients be put off by an AI answering a med spa's phone?

Some callers prefer a human, and the humans on your team should still take every call they can. The comparison that matters is against the alternative, which is voicemail or endless ringing. A natural-sounding receptionist that answers immediately, handles questions, and books the consultation converts far better than a missed call, especially after hours when no spa in town is picking up.
