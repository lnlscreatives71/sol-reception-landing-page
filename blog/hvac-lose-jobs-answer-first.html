Why HVAC companies lose jobs to whoever answers first (and how to stop it)

July 5, 2026 · 7 min read · Sol Reception

A homeowner’s AC quits on the first hot Saturday of the summer. They do not research contractors. They grab their phone, call the first HVAC company that comes up, and if no one answers, they call the next one. The job goes to whoever picks up. Not the best company. Not the cheapest. The one that answered.

That is the uncomfortable truth about running an HVAC business on the phone. Roughly 78% of homeowners hire the first contractor who responds. Speed beats reputation and price in that first moment of contact. So the real question is not whether your crews are good. It is whether anyone is there to answer when the call comes in.

For most HVAC companies, the honest answer is no. And every unanswered ring is a job walking to a competitor.

Why does the first HVAC company to answer usually win the job?

Because a broken furnace or a dead AC unit is an emergency, and emergencies do not wait. When something stops working, the homeowner wants it fixed today, and they will keep dialing until a human helps them. The first company to answer sets the appointment, and the rest never get a callback.

The data backs this up hard. MIT research found that responding within five minutes makes you 21 times more likely to qualify a lead than waiting 30 minutes. ServiceTitan’s benchmark report found that companies answering within two minutes convert 62% of inbound leads, while the industry average, at a 42-minute response time, converts just 28%. For a contractor running 80 leads a month at a $1,400 average job value, that gap works out to around $381,000 a year.

88% of HVAC contractors take longer than five minutes to respond, and the single most common response time is a full day. Speed is the cheapest competitive advantage in the trade, and almost everyone is leaving it on the table.

How many calls does a typical HVAC business actually miss?

More than owners think, because the misses happen exactly when the business is busiest. When your office is staffed, roughly 27% of HVAC calls still go unanswered. When crews are on site and no one is at the desk, that number jumps to 62%.

The reason is simple. Your best people are the ones on the tools, and they cannot stop mid-repair to answer the phone. So the calls stack up during peak season, on weekends, and after hours, which is exactly when 62% of HVAC calls come in. Furnaces fail at 2 AM. AC units die on Sunday afternoons. That is not the exception. That is the pattern.

And callers do not leave a message and wait patiently. About 85% of people who reach voicemail never call back, and 62% call a competitor instead. A missed call is not a delayed job. It is usually a lost one.

What does a missed HVAC call really cost?

Each missed HVAC call represents somewhere between $350 and $1,200 in lost revenue, depending on the job. Miss five to ten calls a week, which is normal during a heat wave or a cold snap, and you are looking at $45,000 to $120,000 a year gone. Not from bad work or bad reviews. From a phone nobody could get to.

Most owners never see this number because it never shows up on a report. There is no line item for the job you never booked. The loss is invisible, which is exactly why it goes unfixed year after year while the business blames slow seasons or the market.

Put a real figure on it. Take your average ticket, multiply by the calls you miss in a busy week, then multiply by the weeks your season actually runs. The result is almost always larger than any single marketing expense you are worried about. You are spending money to make the phone ring, then letting a chunk of those calls hit voicemail.
