Where do your med spa's after-hours consultation requests go?

October 12, 2026 · 5 min read · Sol Reception

It is 8:40 on a Tuesday night. Someone has been scrolling before and after photos for an hour. They finally decide they want to ask about lip filler, or Botox before a wedding, or a CoolSculpting consult. They tap your number.

Your med spa closed at six. The call rings out or lands in voicemail. They do not leave a message. They tap the next spa on the list.

That is the most expensive call your med spa never knew about. Med spa clients make the decision to book in the evening, on their phone, in a short burst of motivation. If the answer is a closed door, the motivation moves to someone else.

Short answer: A med spa can book consultations after hours by using a bilingual AI receptionist that answers every call on the first ring, asks a few intake questions (treatment of interest, new or returning, preferred day), checks the live calendar, and books the consult before the caller hangs up. Sol does this day or night in English and Spanish, and sends the team a clean summary in the morning. Your staff keeps handling the clients in the room while the evening calls stop going to voicemail.

Why do med spa clients call after hours in the first place?

Because that is when they have time to think about themselves. During the workday they are at their jobs, with their kids, or in meetings. Evenings and weekends are when people browse treatments, compare spas, and decide.

Browsing turns into calling fast. A person who has read three reviews and watched two treatment videos is warm. They are ready to ask one question and book one appointment. They are not ready to wait until tomorrow at 9 for a callback, because by tomorrow at 9 the urge has cooled or another spa answered first.

Your phone is busiest with high-intent callers exactly when your front desk is gone.

What does a missed after-hours call actually cost a med spa?

Here is an illustrative example. Swap in your own numbers.

Say your med spa gets 12 calls a month outside business hours. Voicemail catches the call, but only 2 or 3 callers leave a message, and by the time your team calls back the next day, maybe half of those still want to book. Call it 1 booked consult from 12 calls.

If you could answer all 12 and book even half, that is 6 consults. Five extra consults a month. If a consult turns into a treatment worth $450 on average, that is $2,250 a month, or $27,000 a year, from calls you already paid to generate through ads, Google, and word of mouth.

If a client becomes a repeat patient, the number goes up. A first Botox visit often turns into a visit every few months.

You did not lose those clients because your treatments were worse. You lost them because the phone went to voicemail at 8:40 PM.

Why does voicemail fail so badly for med spas?

Voicemail asks the caller to do the work. Leave your name, your number, your question, and wait. For a service where the buyer is comparing three spas on one phone, that is a lot to ask.

Cosmetic treatments are also an emotional purchase. People are a little nervous about asking. They want a friendly voice that makes it easy. A beep and a recording feels like a closed door.

And the numbers back up what you already suspect: a caller who reaches nobody usually moves on within minutes. A callback the next morning arrives after the decision has been made somewhere else.

What should an AI receptionist do when a med spa client calls after hours?

A good one handles the whole first conversation, not just a message. Here is what that looks like in practice.

It answers on the first ring and greets the caller by your spa's name. It asks what treatment they are interested in. It asks if they are new or returning. It offers consult times from your live calendar and books one. It texts a confirmation with the address, prep notes, and your cancellation policy.

It also knows its limits. It does not give medical advice, quote results, or promise outcomes. If someone asks a clinical question, it captures the question and flags it for your provider. If someone describes a reaction to a recent treatment, it gives your urgent-care instructions and alerts your team right away.

That is the difference between an answering machine and a receptionist: the caller leaves the conversation with an appointment, not a to-do for your staff.

Can an AI receptionist handle Spanish-speaking med spa clients?

Yes, and for many spas this is a quiet source of lost revenue. A Spanish-speaking caller who reaches an English-only voicemail often just hangs up. They do not try again.

Sol answers in English or Spanish and switches based on how the caller starts speaking. Intake, booking, and the confirmation text all happen in the language the client is comfortable in. If your market includes Spanish-speaking clients, that is one more group of high-intent callers you stop losing.

What about pricing questions after hours?

Pricing calls are common at night, and they are where voicemail does the most damage. A client asks “how much is Botox?” and gets a recording. They move on to the spa that answered.

With Sol you decide what to share. Many spas give starting prices or a range, explain that final pricing depends on the consult, and then book the consult. The caller feels informed, and your team does not have to repeat the same answer all day. For treatments you do not quote by phone, Sol explains that the provider will tailor the plan and offers the booking.

Will an AI receptionist replace my front desk?

No, and it should not. Your front desk is the warm, human part of your spa. It checks clients in, answers questions face to face, sells packages, and makes people feel taken care of.

Sol backs up that team. It covers the hours when nobody is there, and it covers the busy moments when your front desk is with a client and the phone is ringing. When something needs a person, Sol hands it over with the details already captured. Your team starts the morning with booked consults and clear notes instead of a voicemail pile.

How do you set an AI receptionist up for a med spa?

It is lighter than most owners expect. You give Sol your treatment menu, hours, policies, and booking rules. You connect your calendar or booking tool. You decide what Sol can say about pricing, what it should always hand to a human, and how you want urgent calls handled.

Then you test it by calling it yourself. Most owners find the weak spots in the first few calls and fix them in minutes. After launch, you read the call summaries and tweak as you learn what clients actually ask.

What results should a med spa expect?

Think in terms of outcomes, not features. More consults on the calendar. Fewer voicemails to chase. Less time your front desk spends on repetitive questions. A spa that feels open at 8:40 PM on a Tuesday.

You will not book every caller. Some are just curious, and some will still choose another spa. But the callers who were ready and had nobody to talk to stop slipping away, and those are the easiest bookings you will ever win.
