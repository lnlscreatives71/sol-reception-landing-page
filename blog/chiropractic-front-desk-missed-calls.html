7 patient calls your chiropractic front desk is missing this week

July 7, 2026 · 6 min read · Sol Reception

You are on the table with a patient, hands on their spine, and the phone rings at the empty front desk. It rings out. The person on the other end had acute low back pain and wanted to be seen today. They did not leave a message. They called the chiropractor two blocks over, who picked up.

That is the pattern, and it repeats all week. A chiropractic front desk is one or two people who cannot answer the phone while they are rooming a patient, running insurance, or checking someone out. So calls slip through, and most of them are new patients or bookings you never find out about.

Here is the short version: the calls a chiropractic office misses most are new-patient and same-day booking requests during treatment hours and after you close, and each missed new-patient call costs roughly $300 in expected revenue. An AI receptionist answers every one of them, books straight into your schedule, and hands off to your team when a human is needed. It backs up your front desk, it does not replace it. 01

The acute-pain patient who wants to be seen today

Someone tweaks their back lifting a kid or a couch and calls you in real pain. This is the easiest booking you will get all week, because they want relief now and they are not price shopping. But it usually comes in during your busiest treatment block, when the desk is empty and the phone rings out.

When a new patient reaches a live person and gets offered a slot, 55 to 75 percent book. When they hit voicemail, only 5 to 20 percent do, and most never leave a message. So this is not a maybe. Miss the call and you almost certainly lose the patient. Sol answers on the first ring, offers today’s real openings, and books them before the pain sends them elsewhere.

02

The sciatica call at 9 p.m.

Nerve pain does not keep office hours. It flares in the evening, and that is exactly when a motivated patient sits on the couch and starts calling clinics. Roughly 42 percent of patients prefer to book outside business hours, and clinics that shut the phones off at 5 p.m. leave real money on the table, by some estimates hundreds of thousands a year across a busy practice.

If your line goes to voicemail at night, that 9 p.m. caller books with whoever answers. Sol takes the call live, answers the basic questions, and locks in a morning appointment while your team is home for the night. The patient wakes up already on your schedule.

03

The new patient comparing two clinics

Most new chiropractic patients are calling more than one office. The first practice that answers and offers a clear appointment usually wins them, before the second call is even dialed.

A missed new-patient call for a chiropractor costs about $318 in expected revenue on average, and far more once you count a full care plan and referrals.

Your front desk cannot win that race while they are with a patient in the back. Sol can. It answers instantly every time, so you are the clinic that picked up, not the one that called back an hour later to find they already booked.

04

The reschedule that quietly becomes a no-show

A patient needs to move Thursday’s visit. They call, cannot get through, and instead of leaving a message they just do not show. Now you have an empty slot you cannot fill and a treatment plan that stalls. Missed appointments cost the wider healthcare system billions a year, and every no-show at your clinic is a paid-for slot sitting empty.

Sol answers the reschedule call live, moves the appointment in your system, and frees the slot early enough that someone else can take it. The plan stays on track and the chair stays full.

05

The “do you take my insurance?” call that stalls

A lot of first calls open with an insurance question. If nobody answers, the booking dies right there, because the patient will not chase you for the answer. Even when the desk does pick up during a rush, the caller gets a hurried response and hangs up unsure.

Sol answers the common coverage and pricing questions calmly, then moves the caller straight into booking a first exam instead of leaving them to think about it. The question gets answered, and the appointment gets made in the same call.

06

The Spanish-speaking caller who hits an English-only voicemail

In a lot of US markets a real share of new patients are more comfortable in Spanish. If they call and reach an English-only voicemail, they hang up and try a clinic where they can actually be understood. That is a steady stream of new patients most front desks lose without ever knowing.

Sol answers in both English and Spanish, so a Spanish-speaking caller gets a real conversation and a booked appointment instead of a dead end. For many practices this alone recovers several new patients a month.

07

The lunch-hour and turnover gap

Two predictable holes open in every week. Lunch, when the desk is covered by nobody or by whoever is around, and the stretch after a front desk person quits, when a new hire is still learning your services and schedule. Calls get dropped in both. Front desk turnover is high in this field, and every gap is a run of missed bookings.

Sol does not take lunch, call in sick, or quit. It covers those gaps automatically so a staffing hiccup never turns into a week of lost patients.

What this adds up to

Run the math conservatively. A clinic taking 10 missed calls a day, with 40 percent of them appointment-related, loses about 4 bookings a day. At a modest average visit value, that annualizes into six figures of lost revenue, and that is before care plans, follow-ups, and lifetime patient value. A single new patient on a treatment plan is worth far more than one visit.

None of this means your front desk is failing. It means one or two people cannot be on the table and on the phone at the same time. That is the gap, and it is fixable without adding a salary. Sol answers every call, day and night, in English and Spanish, and books straight into your schedule. Your team keeps doing the hands-on work only they can do, and stops losing patients to a ringing phone.
