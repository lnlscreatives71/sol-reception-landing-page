Why roofing companies lose estimates to whoever calls back first

September 28, 2026 · 5 min read · Sol Reception

A homeowner finds a leak after a storm. They pull up their phone and call three roofing companies inside the same ten minutes. Whoever picks up first gets the inspection scheduled. The other two never hear from that homeowner again, even if their price would have been better.

That is the whole game in residential roofing right now. Homeowners are not loyal to a roofing brand they have never used. They are loyal to whoever answers.

Short answer: Roofing companies lose jobs to competitors mainly because calls go unanswered while crews are on a roof, trucks are between job sites, or the office is closed nights and weekends. The contractor who calls back first typically wins the estimate, and a callback delay of just 30 minutes cuts conversion sharply. How many roofing leads are actually lost to missed calls?

More than most roofing owners want to admit. Industry estimates put missed or voicemail-routed calls at 20 to 30 percent of total inbound volume for a typical roofing company, and roughly 85 percent of homeowners who do not reach someone on the first try will not call back. They simply call the next name on their list.

That is not a minor leak in the pipeline. If a fifth to a third of every call a roofing company gets never turns into a conversation, that is a fifth to a third of the marketing spend, the storm chasing, the door knocking, and the reviews that made the phone ring in the first place, wasted on a line nobody picked up.

Why does the first roofer to answer usually win the job?

Because a roof is not a considered purchase the way a kitchen remodel is. It is urgent, it is stressful, and most homeowners assume one roofer is about as good as the next until proven otherwise. Speed becomes the tiebreaker.

The data backs this up. The first 60 seconds after a homeowner calls are the highest-intent window there is. Per the MIT and InsideSales.com Lead Response Management study, contractors who respond within 5 minutes are orders of magnitude more likely to connect and qualify than those who wait 30 minutes. In competitive metro markets, where 20 to 40 roofing companies can be competing for the same search results, commonly cited trade figures put the share of jobs won by the first contractor to respond at 70 to 80 percent, not because their bid was lowest, but because they showed up first.

Roofing companies do not lose bids on price nearly as often as they lose them on silence.

What does a missed roofing call actually cost?

Run the numbers on a mid-size roofing company and the picture gets uncomfortable fast.

Say the average job, blending small repairs with full replacements, is worth about $9,000. A call that gets answered live and scheduled quickly converts to a booked estimate, and then a signed job, at roughly 30 percent. That puts the expected value of one answered call at about $2,700.

Now say that same company misses 6 calls a week between crews being on roofs, trucks in transit, and calls landing after 5pm or on a Saturday. That is 312 missed calls a year. Even being conservative and assuming only half of those were real, qualified homeowners rather than spam or solicitors, that is 156 missed opportunities. At a 30 percent close rate, that is roughly 47 jobs the company never even got the chance to bid on.

Forty-seven jobs at $9,000 each is about $423,000 in lost revenue a year, from a company that was already spending money to make that phone ring in the first place.
