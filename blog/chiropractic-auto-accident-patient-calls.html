Why chiropractors lose auto accident patients to the office that answers first

October 5, 2026 · 5 min read · Sol Reception

Someone gets rear-ended on the way home. They are sore, shaken, and Googling “chiropractor near me” from the driver's seat or the couch that night. They call the first office. Voicemail. They call the second. Busy. The third picks up, asks two good questions, and books them for tomorrow morning.

That third office just won a patient who may come in for weeks of care. The first two never knew the call happened.

Auto accident patients are some of the most valuable calls a chiropractic office gets, and some of the easiest to lose. They are urgent, they are shopping fast, and they do not wait around for a callback.

Short answer: A chiropractic office should answer every auto accident call live, collect a few key details (when it happened, where it hurts, whether insurance or an attorney is involved), and book the first visit before the caller hangs up. When nobody answers, most injured callers move on to the next office within minutes. A bilingual AI receptionist like Sol picks up every call day or night on the first ring, in English and Spanish, captures those details, and books the visit on your calendar so the patient lands with you instead of the next name on the list.

Why do auto accident patients call more than one chiropractor?

Because they are not loyal to anyone yet. They have never been to your office. They are hurting, they want it handled, and they assume one clinic is about as good as another until someone proves otherwise.

So they do what anyone does under stress. They search, they tap the top results, and they call down the list. A call that rings out or hits voicemail is not a pause in that process. It is a rejection. The caller moves to the next number, and the first office to actually talk to them becomes their office.

This is the same pattern that plays out in roofing, plumbing, and every other urgent category. The difference in chiropractic is the lifetime value. One accident patient can mean a full care plan, not a single visit.

What does a missed accident call actually cost a chiropractic practice?

Here is an illustrative example. Your numbers will differ, so swap in your own.

Say your office gets 6 auto accident calls a month. Your front desk is checking patients in, taking payments, and verifying insurance, so it misses about a third of them. That is 2 calls a month. If half of those callers would have booked, you lose 1 new accident patient a month.

If an accident patient is worth $2,500 over a typical care plan, that is $2,500 a month, or $30,000 a year, walking out the door. That is before referrals, and before the patient who tells their coworker who also got hit that week.

You did not lose them to a worse chiropractor. You lost them to a ringing phone.

Why does the front desk miss these calls in the first place?

It is almost never effort. It is physics. A chiropractic front desk is doing five jobs at once. Someone is at the counter. Someone else is on hold with an insurance company. A patient is asking about a bill. The phone rings in the middle of all of it.

Add the hours problem. Accident calls do not follow business hours. They come in at 7 p.m. after the ER visit, on Sunday morning when the neck stiffness finally hits, or the day after when the soreness sets in. If your office is closed, those calls go to voicemail, and an injured person rarely leaves a message for a clinic they have never met.

What should happen on an auto accident call?

A good accident call is short, calm, and organized. The caller should get a human-sounding voice right away, a few simple questions, and a booked time. Here is what a well-run call looks like:

  1. The caller is greeted immediately on the first ring, not after four rings and a menu.
  2. They are asked when the accident happened and where they are hurting.
  3. They are asked, lightly, whether insurance or an attorney is involved so your team knows what to prepare.
  4. They get offered the earliest real appointment on your calendar and are booked on the spot.
  5. They get a text confirmation with the time, address, and anything to bring.

None of that needs a clinician on the phone. It needs someone, or something, that answers every time and captures the right information. Your team then walks into the visit already knowing the story instead of starting from scratch.

The honest answer depends on your call volume and how many calls you are missing today. Count how many were new patient or accident inquiries.

Every one of those is a lead you paid to generate through your website, your reviews, and your local search presence, and then lost at the last step. Answering them is the cheapest growth available to most practices, because the demand is already calling.

The bottom line

Accident patients do not wait. They call, and whoever answers gets the patient. If that is not you every time, you are handing some of your best new patients to the next office on the list.
