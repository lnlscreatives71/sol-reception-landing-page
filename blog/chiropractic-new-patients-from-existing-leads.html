7 ways to get more new chiropractic patients without spending more on ads

September 14, 2026 · 4 min read · Sol Reception

Most chiropractic practices do not have a lead problem. They have a conversion problem. The inquiries are already coming in through the phone, the website form, and the Google listing, and a predictable share of them never turn into a first visit because nobody got back to them fast enough or at all. Fixing that is cheaper and faster than buying more leads, and here are the seven places the leaks usually are.

01

Answer the phone within five minutes, every time

The Lead Response Management study, run by MIT's James Oldroyd with InsideSales.com, found the odds of qualifying a lead drop 21-fold when the callback slips from five minutes to thirty. That research covers sales leads rather than patients, but someone in pain shops the same way. Plenty of practices take hours, sometimes a full day, to follow up on a web inquiry, and by then the person has already booked with the office down the road.

The reality in a busy chiropractic practice is that five minutes is impossible some of the time. Your CA is checking in a patient, running an insurance verification, and handling the phone all at once, and the doctor is in an adjusting room. The call that comes in during that stretch goes to voicemail. Answering inside five minutes is not a discipline problem, it is a coverage problem, and it needs a coverage solution.

02

Cover the hours when people actually decide to call

Back pain does not respect office hours. A person who slept wrong on Sunday night, or who tweaked something moving furniture on a Saturday, is looking for a chiropractor at 9 PM. That is a person in pain with their credit card and their phone in hand, and they are the easiest new patient you will ever get.

If your line goes to voicemail after 5 PM, you are handing that patient to whichever practice has after-hours coverage. Count how many voicemails land outside your hours in a typical week and multiply by your average new patient value. It is usually a bigger number than owners expect.

03

Answer the insurance question on the first call

Most people who call a chiropractic office for the first time are gauging cost before anything else. Do you take my insurance, what does a first visit cost, do I need a referral. If the person answering cannot give a straight answer, or has to promise a callback, the caller keeps dialing.

You do not need a full benefits verification on the phone. You need a clear, confident answer about which plans you are in network with, what a new patient exam typically runs, and what happens if they are self-pay. Write those answers down as a script so that anyone or anything answering your phone gives the same one.

04

Stop making people call you back during business hours

A voicemail that says “our office hours are Monday through Friday” is asking someone in pain to remember you tomorrow while they are at work. Very few do.

The fix is that every inquiry ends with a booked appointment, not a promise. That applies to the web form too. If someone fills out your contact form at 11 PM, the ideal outcome is that they leave with a Tuesday 8 AM slot on your schedule, not an email saying we will be in touch.

05

Answer in Spanish when the caller speaks Spanish

If your practice sits in a market with a significant Spanish-speaking population, some share of your inbound calls are people who would rather explain their pain in Spanish. Right now those calls likely end at “one moment please” while someone looks for help that is not there.

Bilingual coverage on the phone is one of the least competitive advantages available to a chiropractic practice, because so few offices offer it. The caller who reaches an office that speaks their language does not shop around. They book, and then they tell their family.

06

Call your unconverted leads back before you buy new ones

Look at the last ninety days of inquiries that never became patients. The web forms that went cold, the voicemails nobody returned, the people who called about pricing and disappeared. That list is almost always bigger than the practice owner expects, and it cost you nothing extra.

Those people had a problem when they contacted you. Many of them still have it. A short, non-salesy follow-up call offering a specific appointment time converts a meaningful slice of that list, and it costs a fraction of what a new lead does through paid ads.

07

Make sure your busiest hour is not your worst hour on the phone

Here is the pattern that quietly caps growth in a lot of practices. The busier the office gets, the worse the phone gets answered. Monday morning and the 4 PM to 6 PM window are when the phone tends to be busiest and when your front desk has the least capacity to pick up. So your peak demand hours are your peak leak hours, and the practice plateaus at whatever volume one person at the desk can handle.

Growing past that means the phone stops competing with the patient standing at the counter. Your CA should be able to give full attention to the person in front of them without a ringing phone in their ear, and the caller should still get a real conversation and a booked appointment.

FAQ

Common questions

Why do chiropractic practices lose so many new patient inquiries?

The most common cause is response time. The Lead Response Management study from MIT and InsideSales.com found the odds of qualifying a lead drop 21-fold when the callback slips from five minutes to thirty, and plenty of practices take hours or longer to follow up on a call or a web form. In a busy office, the phone competes with patient care, so calls during peak hours and after close go unanswered and the caller books elsewhere.

What is the fastest way to get more chiropractic patients without more marketing spend?

Convert the inquiries you already receive. Answer every call live, cover evenings and weekends, give a straight answer on insurance and pricing, and end every conversation with a booked appointment instead of a callback promise. Most practices find these fixes produce more new patients than an increase in ad budget would.

Should a chiropractic office answer calls after hours?

Yes, if you can do it without burning out your staff. A significant share of new patient calls come in evenings and weekends because that is when acute back and neck pain flares up. Those callers are high intent and ready to book, and they will call the next practice if they reach voicemail.

How much is one missed call worth to a chiropractic practice?

It depends on your new patient value and care plan average, but the math is straightforward. Multiply your average new patient value by your typical phone-to-booking conversion rate to get the expected value of one answered call, then multiply by the number of calls you miss in a month. For most practices that annual number comes out bigger than they expect.

Does offering Spanish-language phone service help a chiropractic practice?

In markets with a meaningful Spanish-speaking population, yes. Very few chiropractic offices can hold a full intake conversation in Spanish, so a practice that can typically faces little local competition for those patients. Those callers also tend to refer family and friends, which compounds the effect.
